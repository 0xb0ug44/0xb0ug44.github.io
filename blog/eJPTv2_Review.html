I recently passed eJPTv2 with a score of 91%, and this post is my honest feedback on the journey. If you are a student or beginner in offensive security, this certification is a practical way to validate core pentesting skills and build confidence.

Who This Review Is For

This review is for anyone preparing for a first practical certification. eJPTv2 is especially useful if you are learning network and host enumeration, service analysis, and attack-path thinking in lab environments.

My Story

I started this journey in September 2025. I am a self-taught penetration tester with a strong academic background in cybersecurity, currently pursuing an engineering degree in Security of Computer Systems and Networks at TEK-UP. Beyond academics, I actively sharpen my skills through hands-on practice. As a member of the Securinets club, I engage in cybersecurity events, workshops, and technical challenges. I also regularly participate in Capture The Flag (CTF) competitions, where I develop practical expertise in areas such as web exploitation, privilege escalation, and vulnerability analysis.

My main motivation was simple: I wanted to measure my real level. Because eJPTv2 is a hands-on certification, passing it would confirm whether I was actually ready to begin working as a penetration tester.

My 6-Day Journey: Study Tips & Recommendations

My preparation was short and focused. Over six days, I watched lectures at 2x speed while completing labs in parallel. I already knew many of the topics, so I used this time to adapt to the exam style and expectations.

study-plan.md markdown 
- Watch INE lectures (2x speed)
- Complete labs while learning
- Strengthen pivoting workflows
- Practice advanced Nmap usage
- Train brute-force tooling (Hydra or equivalent)
- Use Metasploit and Searchsploit effectively
- Keep structured notes for faster recall

Exam Experience

The exam is 48 hours long, but I completed mine in around 7 hours including food breaks. Taking short breaks helped me avoid burnout and think more clearly when I got stuck. In multiple moments, stepping away for a few minutes helped me return with the right approach.

The exam felt fair and practical. The biggest differentiator was disciplined enumeration. Questions are solvable when you map the environment carefully, prioritize likely attack paths, and avoid rushing into exploitation too early.

What Helped Me Most

The exam rewards structured thinking more than brute forcing tools. What worked best for me was keeping organized notes, verifying each foothold, and reading each question carefully before acting.

Tips for Future Candidates

  • Do not rush. Build an attack path from evidence.
  • Treat enumeration as your primary skill.
  • Track credentials, hosts, and findings in one place.
  • Review what each question is truly asking before submitting.
  • After passing, revisit weak domains and close the gaps.

Final Verdict

eJPTv2 is a strong beginner-to-junior practical certification that covers essential pentesting skills in a realistic way. After completing it, I feel more confident performing structured security assessments and communicating findings clearly. I am glad I took the challenge and passed with 91%.
